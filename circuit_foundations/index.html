    Circuit Foundations🔗

    Every circuit is a push, a flow, and something in the way. This topic builds those three ideas from the atom up, then uses them on everything that follows: power and heat, faults and fuses, alternating current, magnetism, and the two components that store energy.

    Each article stands on its own, but they're written to be read in this order, and each one ends by pointing to the next. No prior knowledge is assumed anywhere. If you're studying for the Canadian amateur radio Basic exam, Start Here on Exploring Radio shows which of these articles each exam topic draws on.

    1. What Electricity Is Made Of🔗

    • Conductors, Insulators, and Semiconductors — why wires are copper, contacts are gold, and insulators are glass and plastic, from the atom up
    • What Is Electricity? — the whole picture on one short page, before the deep dives
    • Metric Prefixes and Units — milli, micro, kilo, and mega, until a reading of 0.025 A and one of 25 mA look like the same thing

    2. The Three Quantities🔗

    • Voltage — energy per charge, always measured between two points
    • Current — charge per second, the same all the way round a loop, and the quantity that hurts
    • Resistance and Conductance — what sets a wire's resistance, tolerance, and why a long extension cord gets warm

    3. Putting Them to Work🔗

    • Ohm's Law and Power — the equation that ties the three together, the heat that follows, and where the law stops holding
    • Open Circuits, Short Circuits, and Fuses — what happens when a circuit goes wrong, and the deliberate weak link that protects it

    4. Beyond Steady DC🔗

    • AC vs DC — current that reverses sixty times a second, sine waves, and what "120 V" really means
    • Magnetism and Electromagnetism — fields, electromagnets, induction, and transformers

    5. Combining and Storing🔗

    • Series and Parallel Circuits — the two ways every circuit is wired, and what each does to voltage and current
    • Voltage Dividers — two resistors that make any voltage you need, and why the output sags under load
    • Capacitors — storing charge, the RC time constant, and how a camera flash makes 15 kW from two AA cells
    • Inductors — coils that resist change, and how a 12 V battery fires a spark plug at 40 kV

    What You'll Take Away🔗

    • The vocabulary

      Volts, amps, ohms, watts, farads, and henries, and the prefixes that scale them.

    • The arithmetic

      Ohm's law, power, series and parallel, and time constants, worked with real values.

    • The judgement

      How much current is safe, why a part gets hot, and what a fuse or a flyback diode is for.

    • The ground for everything else

      Components, microcontrollers, and power all build on this topic.

    Where to Start🔗

    New to electronics? Start at the atom with Conductors, Insulators, and Semiconductors. If you'd rather see the whole picture first, What Is Electricity? covers it on one page and links into each deep dive.
